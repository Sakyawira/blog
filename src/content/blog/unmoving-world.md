They stopped the setting sun from fully hiding behind the horizon. They stopped the scorching plasma erupting from the star from reaching this planet.

By literally stopping time.

But our consciousness still ticks.

The sight of sunset reminds us of nothing but our failings — men and the gods they worship. And so we build a veil of metal pipes around us, because rain no longer falls normally — we pump water from the earth and sprinkle it high above the now-still, unmoving clouds.

Water will fall — cold water. Refreshing, but toxic.

Even if it does not burn our skin, it reminds us too much of the moving world. The cycle. It was important, but it was shattered.

The world is ending.

There is almost no reason for me or the people beside me to do whatever it is we are doing right now.

Exploring the pipes.

Where do the pipes lead?

When we asked this question, something hanging from the ceiling began moving towards us. It talked.

“City of the End, where perpetual sunset hangs upon the sky — poetic, isn’t it? But the reality is that we are stuck — the gods have saved us. They have stopped the calamity — they have stopped the end of the world. No — they literally did.

There, in the middle of the City of the End, is a belfry. Sculpted onto its bell are illustrations of the last of the gods.

General Veridia — the iron valkyrie — and her legion of knights.

Lady Yosefka of the Seas and the Darkmoon.

And the Profaned Dolls’ maker — Luciel the Broken.

Together, they challenged the conqueror of the stars. Their clash caused time and space to seize up.

Until what’s left is the City of the End.”

“Or so I have heard,” said the man with a weird hat and even weirder glasses.

“Wait, so you’re saying you don’t know how to create the potion that can cure vampirism?”

“My friend, if I did, I would not be here — at the literal end of the world. Look, the bright side is that I have looked everywhere else. So if a cure exists, it is here.”
