There, the two of us sat on the shore of the island staring at the panoramic view of the sea. Far from the malicious entities we have been fighting for the last three months or so. The rusty metallic chair we sat on is not the most comfortable yet the amusing lights across the water gives me, at least, a quite cheerful feeling.

“Putra,” she broke the silence. “Is it true?”

“What’s true?”

“That the one thing you want in life — is that for her to come back to life?”

“Yeah,” I answered, turning my face to face her. To my surprise she was no longer sitting with feet on the ground but was squating on top of the chair. She pulled her long red skirt down to cover her toes.

“Cold?” I asked her, not because I was genuinely concerned, but isn’t this just the automatic response when seeing someone half-shivering like that?

“Then, once this temporary partnership ends,” she continued, a follow up to my answer, and not my question. “I will have to kill you.”

“You can try,” I answered with a serious tone. The girl that was looking straight to the other side of the strait was so surprised with sudden change of demeanor that she tilt her whole body to face me. She looked at me with an intense yet curious expression.

“Jokes, we both know you can kill me easily,” I smiled. “Make it painless, won’t you?”

Her surprised expression faded, and morph into a mix of annoyance and disappointment.

“I didn’t know it can be this cold in Singapore, I guess it’s just the wind?” She mumbled quietly, almost like a whisper, not sure if the question was meant to be rhetoric.

“Then give it up. Give me your key ,” she stuck her hand out towards me, palms up.

“If you don’t mind, I would like to hold on to it till my last moment,” I closed her palms and got up from the seat. I walked closer to the shore and she followed behind me. She noticed that there were ferris going out to the island across the strait. “I wonder why they still use the ferris to go to the mainland when they have a fully functional bridge.”

“That is not the mainland,” I corrected her. “That is not even Singapore anymore.”

“Huh? Then what is it?”

“Home.”
