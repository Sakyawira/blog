This is an amateur, values-driven restructuring of New Zealand's tax, labour, education, housing, and welfare systems. It replaces personal income tax with a phased-in land value tax, tiered GST, tariffs, and a set of integrity levies (fringe benefits tax, securities-backed loan tax), while retaining a reinstated corporate/company tax (0% to $1M profit, 25% above); restructures secondary education around corporate-funded training; sends a share of students abroad on sponsored study-and-work exchanges; replaces state pensions and healthcare with mandatory non-profit insurance; and introduces a 75% inheritance tax.

The guiding value underlying the design remains explicit: generational wealth transfer is treated as something to discourage rather than protect, in favour of each generation building and spending down its own position.

## The Full Policy Package

### Tax Structure

- No personal income tax — wages and salaries are entirely untaxed.
- Corporate/company tax (reinstated): 0% on annual company profit up to $1M, 25% flat on profit above $1M. Entities are consolidated for the purpose of calculating the $1M threshold using the same direct-control test applied in the Labour Market Rules — two or more companies are treated as one entity if one controls the other's operations, staffing, or strategic decisions, regardless of recorded ownership percentage. This closes the gap a pure ownership-percentage test would leave open (nominee shareholders, ownership spread across family members or trusts to stay under a control threshold while operations remain centrally run).
- National Land Value Tax (LVT): collected by central government, no owner-occupier exemption, phased in over 5 years to give the market time to adjust — 1% in year 1, rising by 1 percentage point each year, reaching a permanent 5% from year 6 onward. As land values are expected to soften in response to the tax (and to the home-equity drawdown mechanism), the actual dollar bill in later years may rise more slowly than the rate schedule alone would suggest, or even fall in real terms if price declines outpace the rate increases.
- Apartments and other unit-titled properties: land value is assessed once for the whole site and apportioned across individual units by ownership interest, using the same method NZ's valuers already apply for rates today. Because land value is shared across many units, an apartment's LVT bill is typically far lower in absolute terms than a standalone house of similar total property value — a strong, organic incentive toward higher-density housing that reinforces the tax's underlying purpose.
- Local council rates: reformed to apply only to Value of Improvements (VI) — the building/development value, excluding land — multiplied by 0.1% per resident occupying the property. A 4-person household in a $500k-improvement property pays 0.1% × 4 × $500k = $2,000/year; a single occupant in the same property pays $500/year.
- Tiered GST: 0% on groceries, clothing under $200/item, residential housing, and scheduled public transport (including services like the TranzAlpine); 30% standard rate on everything else; ~45-50% premium rate on short-term/hotel accommodation, tour and travel-company products, and luxury goods priced 5x or more over the average cost of an equivalent product.
- Import tariffs: 10% default on all imports from non-FTA-partner countries, with an exemption process (no domestic provider exists); tariff is payable unless and until an exemption is approved. FTA-partner-origin goods (China, Australia, and other treaty partners) are excluded, as required by existing trade agreements.
- Subsurface volume levy (below 5m depth, state-owned): ~$1,000/m³/year for construction/occupation (basements, carparks, foundations); a deliberately prohibitive rate for mining/extraction footprint, functioning as de facto nationalisation; negligible in practice for water (bore-shaft volume only).
- Fringe benefits tax: 50% on the value of non-cash assets given as compensation, above $1M, charged once at the time of transfer. The full asset value counts toward the 5x pay-ratio calculation for that period.
- Securities-backed loan tax: 50% on new borrowing drawn within a financial year against financial securities/equity, above a threshold of roughly NZD 2–3 million (approx. double mean NZ net worth, recalculated periodically). Resets annually — does not accumulate against a lifetime cap. Explicitly excludes mortgages, business operating loans, and ordinary consumer credit. Requires anti-fragmentation rules aggregating loans across related entities/trusts for the same beneficial owner.
- 75% inheritance tax, applied broadly, without a large exemption threshold for modest estates.
- Trust beneficial-ownership disclosure, replacing an outright ban, to close inheritance-tax avoidance while preserving legitimate uses (minors, disabled beneficiaries, charities).
- Tourism levies: raised international visitor arrival levy, higher national park/great walk access fees for foreign visitors, cruise-ship per-passenger port levies.

### Labour Market Rules

- 5x maximum pay ratio between the highest- and lowest-paid employee at any entity, using a direct-control test to define "employee" (closing shell-company and outsourcing workarounds while excluding genuine contractors and franchisees).
- Mandatory equal bonus distribution — any bonus pool split equally per employee.
- Ministers, MPs, and senior public-service "chief" level salaries reduced to median NZ wage (adopted primarily for legitimacy/consistency with the 5x cap; fiscally negligible).

### Welfare Replacement

- Mandatory non-profit health insurance — Swiss/Dutch-style, community-rated, with risk equalisation between insurers.
- Mandatory pension accounts — Chile-style individual accounts, with regulatory guardrails on fees and a minimum-return backstop.
- Mandatory non-profit unemployment insurance — Ghent-system style, with a government fiscal backstop for systemic recessions.
- Residual, tax-funded welfare retained for categories that cannot be insured: disability support, Working for Families-style child payments, housing assistance, sole-parent support, and jobseeker support for those without a contribution history.

### Housing-Specific Rules

- Rent stabilisation: 5% annual increase cap tied permanently to the housing unit (not the tenancy) — landlords cannot reset pricing via eviction or new contracts, enforced by a tenancy authority.
- New-build starting rents pegged to the regional average for that housing type, plus the prior year's inflation rate.
- Mutual tenancy bond: 4 weeks' rent from tenant and landlord alike (8 weeks total); the landlord's bond is forfeited to the tenant if an eviction or rent increase is found unfair.
- Elderly home-equity mechanism (retirees, primary residence, 183+ days/year): unpaid LVT after 183 days automatically triggers a home-reversion-style equity draw (percentage-of-value sale, no compounding interest, no negative equity, lifetime occupancy guaranteed). LVT liability is split proportionally with any lender/investor holding a share, creating an incentive to draw down progressively over time. Draws are intended to be pooled and securitised, sold to domestic (KiwiSaver) and international institutional investors.

### Education Restructuring

- Ages 12–18: a 5-hour school day — 2 hours of core academic content (maths, science, one foreign language of choice, civics, and one sport of choice) plus 3 hours of corporate-run, non-productive training (genuine skills instruction, not billable work), rotating every 3 months across 12 different placements over the 6 years (software, mechanical, agriculture, hospitality, fishing, healthcare-adjacent, etc.).
- A national coordinating institutional body (modelled on Germany/Switzerland's industry chambers and NZ's existing Industry Training Organisations) allocates rotations for sector variety, certifies and audits training quality, and enforces the firewall between genuine training and productive labour.
- Ages 16–22 (optional, alongside the above): a share of students take a fully sponsored overseas study-and-work programme, funded by partner countries facing population decline, studying and then working under contract before returning to New Zealand; students are free to return to the host country permanently after a domestic work period, with no enforced lifetime return mandate. The programme's fiscal case rests on education-cost savings and diaspora remittance/investment, not on guaranteed workforce return.

### Growth Strategy

- Zero personal income tax, paired with a 0% rate on the first $1M of company profit and 25% above that, positioned as a deliberate pitch to attract globally mobile high earners, remote workers, and businesses considering redomiciling.
- Fast-tracked skilled-migration visas for doctors and renewable-energy engineers, reusing existing domestic licensing exams (the same exam sat by NZ-trained graduates) gated by an English-language test and a prior-qualification screen, piloted with doctors first.
- Long-term industrial strategy options: green hydrogen export (leveraging NZ's hydro/geothermal capacity), data-centre/AI-compute hosting, and positioning NZ as a financial hub for the new securitised home-equity and infrastructure-bond asset classes.

## How the Policies Interact

### Reinforcing Combinations

- Flat LVT + VI/occupancy-based local rates cleanly separate national and local tax bases with no double-taxation, while tying local funding to actual service demand (people, not just property value).
- Fringe benefits tax + securities-backed loan tax together close the two main ways a wealthy individual could otherwise avoid all taxation entirely under a no-income-tax, no-capital-gains-tax system: in-kind compensation, and living off borrowed money against untaxed equity.
- Annually re-indexed LVT + home-reversion equity draws remove the negative-equity and margin-call risk that a fixed-debt reverse mortgage would create for fixed-income owners facing the flat LVT.
- Direct-control employee test + 5x pay cap + fringe benefits tax together close shell-company gaming, in-kind compensation gaming, and outsourcing gaming of the pay-ratio rule simultaneously.
- Corporate 12–18 training programme + fast-tracked skilled migration build a domestic pipeline and reinforce it with an external one, both aimed at the same underlying goal: a highly-skilled, well-matched workforce.

### Tensions and Unresolved Risks

- The LVT's destination rate (5%, reached in year 6) remains the single largest uncertainty in the system, though the 5-year phase-in substantially improves the picture. A year-1 bill of roughly 1% on an average property (land value $400–500k) is approximately $4,000–5,000 — a real but manageable cost, closer in scale to a significant rates increase than a household-threatening shock. If land values soften over the phase-in period as intended (both from the tax itself and from the home-equity drawdown mechanism releasing supply), the year-6 dollar bill at the full 5% rate could land well below what 5% on today's values would suggest. The main residual risk is a scenario where land values stay comparatively sticky while the rate keeps climbing on schedule — in which case the final year's step-up could still land closer to the harder $20,000+ scenario for standalone houses on high land value. Building in a data-triggered pause (delaying the final step if median land values have not softened materially by year 3–4) would further reduce this risk.
- Apportioned land value for apartments and unit-titled housing means the LVT lands far more lightly on higher-density living than on standalone houses of similar total value — a genuinely positive, self-reinforcing interaction with the tax's underlying purpose, and one that naturally reduces exposure for retirees and lower-income households already living in higher-density housing.
- Reinstating corporate tax (0% to $1M, 25% above) closes what was identified repeatedly as the single largest gap in the system — a company's profit is now taxed annually, at the point it is earned, regardless of whether the owner ever takes salary, borrows against shares, or dies. This is a much stronger revenue base than the fringe benefits tax and securities-backed loan tax alone, which remain useful as backstops for the residual cases (in-kind compensation, large borrowing) but are no longer the only mechanisms touching business-derived wealth.
- Securities-backed lending remains the lightest-taxed way to hold wealth even after the new loan tax — a patient holder who never borrows above the ~$2–3M annual threshold and never sells faces close to zero ongoing tax exposure, while a landholder faces the full flat 5% LVT every year regardless of income. This continues to push sophisticated wealth away from land and toward equity.
- Overseas study/work exchange no longer has an enforced return mechanism — the fiscal case shifted deliberately from a workforce-return saving to a smaller, slower, less certain diaspora-remittance benefit, since an enforceable international return mandate was assessed as impractical and prone to undermining the host country's incentive to participate at all.
- Permanent per-unit rent caps and mandatory training/apprenticeship obligations on companies both risk uneven compliance quality if mandated uniformly across all businesses regardless of size or fit — both work better as strongly incentivised, audited systems than as blanket mandates.

### The Core Unresolved Question

The system commits to zero personal income tax and a light-touch corporate tax (0% to $1M, 25% above), funded by land, consumption, a reinstated corporate tax, and a set of integrity levies. This is a genuinely novel combination with no exact real-world precedent, and its success depends heavily on two things going right simultaneously: the phased LVT being absorbed by the equity-share/securitisation market without destabilising the land value base, and the growth strategy (skilled migration, zero personal-tax positioning, export growth) generating enough new economic activity to expand the GST, corporate tax, and land tax base faster than any capital or population flight shrinks it.

## Key Open Questions and Risks

- Whether the 5% destination rate remains fiscally and socially sustainable once the phase-in completes in year 6, or whether a data-triggered pause (delaying the final step if land values haven't softened materially) should be built into the schedule from the outset.
- Whether the reinstated corporate tax's $1M threshold and consolidation rule are robust enough against entity-splitting, and whether a capital gains tax on share sales is still worth adding to close the narrower remaining gap (patient, low-distribution equity holders who reinvest under the threshold and never sell).
- Whether occupancy-based local rates can be reliably verified given NZ's current lack of an annual household-occupancy registration system.
- What government capacity and capital commitment would be required if the state chooses to run mining operations itself following the effective exit of private operators.
- Whether foreign participation in securitised home-equity instruments should be limited to certain tranches, reserving others for domestic retirement funds (KiwiSaver).
- How gig-economy work is treated under the direct-control employee test, still a genuinely unresolved edge case internationally.
